US bank supervisors are asking about AI agents. Could you answer?

On 13 October 2026 Reuters reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that supervisors at the Federal Reserve, the OCC and the FDIC have been asking banks about the AI agents they use.

As reported, the questions cover the limits set on these systems, who monitors and approves what they do, whether the bank can track and audit an agent's decisions, how an agent is kept within its authority, whether people stay involved in key decisions, and how the bank would shut an autonomous system down. The agencies declined to comment. Nothing has been published: the supervisors are reported to be gathering information, not prescribing practice. Their revised model risk guidance of April 2026 leaves generative and agentic AI out of its scope.

Questions asked informally tend to come back formally. If a supervisor asked you tomorrow to show a decision your agent made last month, who approved it and whether it stayed within its limits, could you?

This is a reading of public reporting, not legal advice.
