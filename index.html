01 -- What it is

Proof of what your AI agents did — that your customer can verify without trusting us.

Your agent's logs are your own testimony.

If you sell AI agents to enterprises, your customer will ask what the agent did and who allowed it. ActaSeal sits in front of the actions that move money. It decides each one it is asked about, against your policy, before it runs -- allow, block, or send to a person for approval -- records the decision in a tamper-evident ledger, and signs a receipt for it. Your customer checks that receipt on their own machine, with an open-source verifier, years later if need be, without trusting us.

pip install actaseal-verify Book a demo

03 -- Why the proof holds

Two things that do not depend on trusting us

Your proof still verifies if we disappear. The verifier is open source under the Apache License 2.0 and makes no network call. A receipt, the slice of the ledger it came from and the public key are all it needs.

Records live outside the agent, so a compromised agent cannot rewrite them. The ledger belongs to the gateway, a separate service from your agent. The agent can ask for a decision; it cannot write the record, and every entry is hash-chained to the one before, so an edit after the fact breaks the chain. The gateway decides about the actions it is asked about -- what ActaSeal is not says where that stops.

04 -- Also used for

Records that have to be provably unaltered

The same signed, independently checkable record is used where a file must be shown not to have changed after a date: audit workpaper archives under PCAOB AS 1215, and records under legal hold, with an FRE 902(13)/(14) certification package for records produced by an electronic process.

These are the standards ActaSeal's exports are built to address. ActaSeal is not certified under any of them; certification, where applicable, is the customer's own process. What ActaSeal is not.
