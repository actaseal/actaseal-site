Security

For your vendor-risk review

What a bank's or a payment company's security and vendor-risk team usually asks first, in one place.

Where it runs
Inside your own environment: one Linux machine or container. Your payment data does not leave it.
Who holds the keys
You do. ActaSeal never holds a production signing key; you choose where it is kept.
Who else sees your data
Your payment records stay with you. The only outside services ActaSeal uses are for billing, email, this website and its domain, and none of them receives your payment records. Sub-processors
What you can check without ActaSeal
Any record, with the free public checker. It does not need an account, the product or ActaSeal's word.
Under NDA
Completed CAIQ-Lite and SIG-Lite questionnaires, and the full signed trust bundle behind the trust page. Ask sales@actaseal.com.

Reporting a vulnerability

If you believe you have found a security issue in ActaSeal, in the standalone verifier, or on this site, please write to security@actaseal.com. A machine-readable version of this contact is published at /.well-known/security.txt.

What to include

What you found, where you found it, and the steps to reproduce it. If the issue concerns a signed artifact, the packet itself is more useful than a description of it. Please do not include third-party data.

What happens next

You will get a reply acknowledging the report. If the issue is confirmed, you will be told what the fix is and when it lands; if it is not, you will be told why. Fixes to the verifier are published in the public repository, so you can check the fix yourself rather than take our word for it.

What this is not

There is no bug bounty and no monetary reward, and this page does not promise a response time it cannot keep.

Scope

In scope: actaseal.com, verify.actaseal.com, the published verifier, and the ActaSeal product itself. Out of scope: findings that require access to a deployment you do not control, reports generated by automated scanners without a demonstrated impact, and issues in third-party services listed on the sub-processors page, which should be reported to those providers.
